\item[Problem 7.1:]
Let $G=(V,E)$ be an unweighted graph with $n$ vertices and $m$ edges ($m\ge n$).
A \emph{loop} of length $\ell\ge 2$ refers to a walk $\langle v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_\ell,v_1\rangle$
such that $v_1,v_2,\ldots,v_\ell$ are all distinct and $v_1v_2,v_2v_3,\ldots,v_\ell v_1\in E$.  Note that a loop is the same as a (simple) cycle, except for the case of $\ell=2$ (usually $\langle v_1,v_2,v_1\rangle$ is not considered a cycle, but is a loop of length 2 if $v_1v_2\in E$).
We study the following problem: find a vertex-disjoint collection of loops and paths, maximizing their total length.

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] (20 pts) Show that there always exists an optimal solution using only loops of length 2 or odd lengths, and no paths.  (So, we can redefine the problem without paths and the problem is the same.)  
\item[(b)] (10 pts) If $G$ is bipartite, show that the optimal total length is the same as $2k^*$, where $k^*$ is the maximum matching size.  (So, the problem is equivalent to maximum matching in the bipartite case.)
\item[(c)] (70 pts) Now consider the problem in the general case when $G$ is not bipartite.
Show that the problem can also be reduced to bipartite maximum matching and so
can be solved in $O(mn)$ time by the Hungarian method or $O(m\sqrt{n})$ time by
Hopcroft and Karp's algorithm.

(Hint: Construct a new bipartite graph $G'$ where we create two versions of each vertex and
two versions of each edge\ldots\ \ Carefully prove correctness.)
\end{enumerate}

\newpage
\textbf{Solution:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(a)]
  \textbf{Even-Length Loops (\(\ell \ge 4\))}:

  Suppose an optimal solution contains an even-length loop \( C = \langle v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{2k}, v_1 \rangle \) where \( k \ge 2 \). Since the vertices are distinct, we can decompose \( C \) into \( k \) disjoint 2-loops:
  \[
  \langle v_1, v_2, v_1 \rangle, \langle v_3, v_4, v_3 \rangle, \dots, \langle v_{2k-1}, v_{2k}, v_{2k-1} \rangle.
  \]
  The original loop has \( 2k \) edges, and the decomposed loops also contribute exactly \( 2k \) edges. Thus, replacing \( C \) with these 2-loops preserves optimality while eliminating even-length loops (\(\ell \ge 4\)).

  \textbf{Paths}:

  Assume an optimal solution contains a path \( P = \langle v_1, v_2, \dots, v_{k+1} \rangle \) with \( k \) edges. 
  \begin{itemize}
    \item If \( v_1 \) and \( v_{k+1} \) are adjacent, we can form a loop of length \( k+1 \), increasing the total edge count from \( k \) to \( k+1 \). This contradicts the optimality of the original solution, so such a path cannot exist in an optimal solution.
    \item If \( v_1 \) and \( v_{k+1} \) are not adjacent, consider the number of vertices \( k+1 \):
    \begin{itemize}
      \item If \( k+1 \) is even, we can pair the vertices into \( \frac{k+1}{2} \) 2-loops, contributing \( k+1 \) edges, which is better than the original \( k \) edges thus cannot exist.
      \item If \( k+1 \) is odd, we can leave \( v_{k+1} \) unused and construct \( \frac{k}{2} \) 2-loops, which does not decrease total length \( k \) and preserves optimality.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
  Thus, even-length loops and paths can always be replaced by 2-loops without decreasing the total length, so they do not appear in an optimal solution.

  \item[(b)]
  Since the vertices of a bipartite graph can be partitioned into two independent sets \( A \) and \( B \), every edge connects \( A \) and \( B \). If an odd-length cycle existed, its start and end vertices would have to belong to the same set, which is not allowed in bipartite graph. Therefore, all cycles in a bipartite graph must have even lengths. According to part (a), we can conclude that the optimal solution consists solely of vertex-disjoint 2-loops.
  
  Each 2-loop corresponds to a matching edge, contributing a length of 2. A collection of vertex-disjoint loops corresponds to a matching \( M \), where the number of edges is \( |M| \), and the total length is \( 2|M| \). Thus, the maximum matching \( k^* \) yields a total length of \( 2k^* \), which is the theoretical maximum.  

  Assume there exists a solution with a total length \( L > 2k^* \). Then, the corresponding matching size would be \( |M| = L/2 > k^* \), contradicting the fact that \( k^* \) is the maximum matching size. Therefore, the optimal total length must be \( 2k^* \).  

  \item[(c)]
  \textbf{Construct the Bipartite Graph \( G' \):}

  Split each vertex \( v \in V \) into two copies \( v^L \) and \( v^R \), placing them in separate partitions \( A \) and \( B \) of \( G' \) so that \( A = \{v^L \mid v \in V\} \) and \( B = \{v^R \mid v \in V\} \).

  For each edge \( (u,v) \in E \), add two edges in \( G' \):
  \( (u^L, v^R) \) and \( (v^L, u^R) \).

  An odd-length \( 2k+1 \) cycle in \( G \)
  \[
  v_1 \to v_2 \to v_3 \to \dots \to v_{2k+1} \to v_1
  \]
  forms a matching of size \( 2k+1 \) in \( G' \):
  \[
  v_1^Lv_2^R, v_2^Lv_3^R, v_3^Lv_4^R, \dots, v_{2k+1}^Lv_1^R.
  \]

  A 2-loop \( \langle u, v, u \rangle \) corresponds to selecting 
  both \( u^Lv^R \) and \( v^Lu^R \) in \( G' \). 

  \textbf{Proof of Correctness:}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[i.] From \( G \) to \( G' \):

    Every vertex-disjoint collection of loops in \( G \) (length-2 or odd) maps to a matching in \( G' \)

    For a loop (length-2 or odd), match points alternately in \( G' \), as stated above.

    The total length in \( G \) is the size of the matching in \( G' \).

    \item[ii.] From \( G' \) to \( G \):

    Every matching in \( G' \) maps back to a vertex-disjoint collection of loops in \( G \):

    A matching of alternating edges 
    \[
    v_1^Lv_2^R, v_2^Lv_3^R, v_3^Lv_4^R, \dots, v_n^Lv_1^R
    \]
    corresponds to a loop 
    \[
    v_1\to v_2\to v_3\to \dots \to v_n \to v_1
    \]
    in \( G \). From part (a), this is equivalent to a collection of vertex-disjoint
    2-loops and odd-length loops.

    A matching
    \[
    v_1^Lv_2^R, v_2^Lv_3^R, v_3^Lv_4^R, \dots, v_{n-1}^Lv_n^R
    \]
    corresponds to a path
    \[
    v_1\to v_2\to v_3\to \dots \to v_n
    \]
    in \( G \). 
    If such a matching exists in the maximum matching of \(G'\), 
    it implies that the vertices \(v_n^L\) and \(v_1^R\) are unmatched. 
    However, by the construction of \(G'\), there must exist an edge \(v_n^Lv_1^R\), 
    which could have been matched, resulting in a loop in \(G\). 
    Hence, path cannot exist in \(G'\)'s maximum matching.

    The property of matching guarantees that no vertex is used twice thus \( G \) is vertex-disjoint.
  \end{enumerate}
  Therefore, the maximum matching of \( G' \) yields the maximum total length of \( G \).
  \( G' \) has \( 2n \) vertices and \( 2m \) edges, 
  which can be solved in $O(mn)$ time by the Hungarian method 
  or $O(m\sqrt{n})$ time by Hopcroft and Karp's algorithm.

\end{enumerate}

\item[Problem 7.2:]
Given an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ and an integer $d$, 
we want determine whether it is possible to direct the edges so that the resulting directed graph has maximum {\bf out-degree\/} at most $d$.
Describe how to solve this problem by reduction to maximum flow.
Prove correctness of your method.

Example: for the undirected graph below (left) and $d=2$, the answer is yes, and one solution is shown on the right (there are many
other solutions).

\fig{hw7fig2}{1}

(Hint: start with a bipartite graph where the vertices on the left side are \emph{edges} in $G$ and the vertices on the right side are the vertices in $G$;
add a source and a sink\ldots)

\newpage
\textbf{Solution:}

\textbf{Construct the Bipartite Graph:}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item On the left side, create one node \(e\) for each edge \(e \in E\) of the original graph \(G\).  
  \item On the right side, create one node \(v\) for each vertex \(v \in V\) of the original graph \(G\).
  \item Add a source node \(s\) and a sink node \(t\).
  \item For each edge node \(e\) on the left, add an arc \((s \to e)\) of capacity \(1\), representing the need to pick exactly one direction for that edge. 
  \item Suppose the edge \(e\) in \(G\) connects vertices \(u\) and \(v\), add arcs \((e \to u)\) and \((e \to v)\), each with capacity \(1\). 
  This enforces that the 1 unit of flow from edge \(e\) can be sent either from vertex \(u\) or from vertex \(v\), 
  but not both simultaneously (by the integrality theorem).
  \item For each vertex \(v\) on the right side, add an arc \((v \to t)\) of capacity \(d\). 
  The capacity \(d\) enforces that at most \(d\) edges can flow \textbf{out} of vertex \(v\).
  \item Compute the maximum flow using Ford-Fulkerson. 
  \item If there is a flow of value \(\lvert E\rvert\), that means we managed to push 1 unit of flow for every edge node to exactly one of its two endpoints, without exceeding the capacity \(d\) at any vertex.  Hence we get a valid orientation with out-degree \(\le d\).  
  \item If the max flow is less than \(\lvert E\rvert\), then no assignment of directions can keep every vertex's out-degree \(\le d\).  
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proof of Correctness:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[1.] If there is an orientation of \(G\) with out-degree \(\le d\), we can construct a flow of value \(\lvert E\rvert\):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item For each edge \(e = \{u,v\}\), suppose it is oriented \(u \to v\).  Then send 1 unit of flow from \(s\) to edge node \(e\), and then from \(e\) to the vertex \(u\) in the flow network.  
    \item Because each vertex \(v\) has at most \(d\) edges oriented out of it in the original orientation, it receives flow from at most \(d\) of its edges.  Hence the flow into \(v\) is at most \(d\), so the capacity \((v \to t)\) is never exceeded.  
    \item Thus, we achieve an integer flow of size exactly \(\lvert E\rvert\).  
  \end{itemize}
\item[2.] If the max flow in the constructed network is \(\lvert E\rvert\), then each edge node \(e\) sends exactly 1 unit of flow to exactly one of its endpoints in \(G\).  That determines a direction of \(e\).  Since no vertex node \(v\) can exceed capacity \(d\) to the sink, at most \(d\) edges are directed out of any vertex \(v\).  
\end{enumerate}

Hence the flow network construction is both sound (no invalid orientations can achieve full flow) and complete (every valid orientation induces a full flow).

\item[Problem 7.3:]
We are given a set of $n$ axis-aligned rectangles $R=\{r_1,\ldots,r_n\}$, where each rectangle $r_i$ has a cost $a_i$.  The rectangles $r_i$ all have height~1 and have integer $x$- and $y$-coordinates, and no two rectangles intersect except along the boundaries, and their union is a rectangle $U(R)$.
We want to redraw the rectangles as $R'=\{r_1',\ldots,r_n'\}$, whose union
is a new rectangle $U(R')$, so that  the rectangles $r_i'$ still have
height~1 and have integer $x$- and $y$-coordinates, and 
the adjacency structure is unchanged, i.e., if a side of the
rectangle $r_i$ touches a side of rectangle $r_j$,
then the corresponding side of $r_i'$ touches the corresponding side of $r_j'$.

Describe a polynomial-time algorithm to compute a redrawing $S'$ that minimizes $\sum_{i=1}^n a_i\cdot\textrm{width}(r_i')$, where $\textrm{width}(r_i')$ denotes
the width of $r_i'$.

You may assume that there is a polynomial-time algorithm for the
following version of the \emph{minimum-cost flow} problem: given
a directed graph $G=(V,E)$ with source $s$ and sink $t$ and a value $d$, where
each edge $e\in E$ is given numbers $\ell(e),c(e),\textrm{cost}(e)$,
compute a flow $f$ of value $d$, minimizing $\sum_{e\in E} \textrm{cost}(e)\cdot f(e)$, such that for every $e\in E$, we have $\ell(e)\le f(e)\le c(e)$, and we have conservation of flow at every vertex in $V-\{s,t\}$.

(Hint: create a vertex for each rectangle\ldots) 

\fig{hw7fig3}{1}

\newpage
\textbf{Solution:}

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Optimal Rectangle Redrawing using Minimum-Cost Flow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
  \Require A set of rectangles \( R = \{r_1, r_2, \dots, r_n\} \), each with height 1, integer coordinates, and cost \( a_i \).
  \Ensure A redrawing \( R' \) that minimizes \( \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \cdot \text{width}(r_i') \).

  \For{each rectangle \( r_i \) in \( R \)}
    \State Add a vertex \( v_i \) to \( V \).
  \EndFor
  \State Add source \( s \) and sink \( t \) to \( V \).

  \For{each rectangle \( r_i \) in the first row}
    \State Add edge \( (s, v_i) \) to \( E \).
  \EndFor

  \For{each row in R except the last row}
    \For{each rectangle \( r_i \) in the row}
      \For{each rectangle \( r_j \) in the next row}
        \If{\( r_i \) and \( r_j \) share a side}
          \State Add edge \( (v_i, v_j) \) to \( E \).
        \EndIf
      \EndFor
    \EndFor
  \EndFor

  \For{each rectangle \( r_i \) in the last row}
      \State Add edge \( (v_i, t) \) to \( E \).
  \EndFor

  \For{each rectangle \( r_i \)}
      \State Set lower bound \( \ell(v_i) = 1 \).
      \State Set upper bound \( c(v_i) = \text{width}(r_i) \).
      \State Set cost \( \text{cost}(v_i) = a_i \).
  \EndFor

  \State Set demand \( d \) to the total width of \( U(R) \).
  \State Compute flow \( f \) minimizing \( \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \cdot \text{width}(r_i') \).
  \State Redraw each rectangle with \( \text{width}(r_i') \).
  \State \Return Redrawing \( S' \).
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Proof of correctness:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Since we have conservation of flow at every vertex in $V-\{s,t\}$, 
  the redrawing \( S' \) remains a rectangle.
  \item Each rectangle width remains within valid bounds, i.e., 
  \( 1 \le \text{width}(r_i') \le \text{width}(r_i) \)
  \item Step \(7\sim11\) guarantees that the new layout maintains the adjacency structure.
\end{itemize}

Creating the directed graph takes polynomial time, computing the minimum-cost flow takes
polynomial time. Thus, the overall algorithm runs in polynomial time.
